faq:
  - q: What is the difference between a life annuity and a term-certain annuity?
    a: A life annuity pays you for as long as you live. A term-certain annuity pays for a fixed number of years, such as 10 or 20, or to a certain age, and then stops. If you pass away before the term ends, the remaining payments go to your beneficiary.
  - q: What is a guarantee period?
    a: A guarantee period, such as 10 or 15 years, makes sure payments continue for at least that long. If you die before it ends, your beneficiary receives the remaining payments or their value. It lowers your payment slightly but protects your family.
  - q: How are annuity payments taxed?
    a: Payments from a registered annuity bought with RRSP or RRIF money are fully taxable as income. For a non-registered annuity, only the interest portion is taxed. A prescribed annuity spreads that interest evenly over the payments, which often means less tax in the early years.
  - q: What is a deferred annuity?
    a: With a deferred annuity, you buy now but payments start later, for example at age 70 or 75. This can lock in future income and act as insurance against outliving your savings.

An annuity is a contract with an insurance company that turns your savings into a **guaranteed income stream**. You pay a lump sum once, and the insurer sends you regular payments, for life or for a set period. It works a lot like creating your own personal pension.

Because payments are guaranteed by the insurer, an annuity removes two big retirement worries: a market crash shrinking your savings, and living longer than your money lasts.

## Choosing the right annuity

Annuities can be shaped to fit your needs. The main choices are:

- **Life or term-certain:** income for your whole life, or for a fixed number of years.
- **Single or joint and survivor:** income for you alone, or for as long as you or your spouse is alive.
- **Guarantee period:** makes sure payments continue to your beneficiary if you pass away early.
- **Indexing:** payments that rise each year to help keep up with the cost of living.
- **Immediate or deferred:** payments that start right away, or at a later date you choose.

## Registered and non-registered annuities

You can buy an annuity with money from an RRSP, RRIF or pension, which makes it a **registered annuity**, and the payments are taxed as regular income. If you use savings that have already been taxed, it is a **non-registered annuity**, and only the interest part of each payment is taxed.
