faq:
  - q: How much TFSA room do I have?
    a: Your room is the sum of each year's limit since you turned 18 (or since 2009, when TFSAs began), plus last year's withdrawals, minus your contributions. The easiest way to see your exact number is in CRA My Account.
  - q: What happens if I over-contribute?
    a: The CRA charges a tax of 1% per month on the highest excess amount in each month, until it is withdrawn or new room becomes available. Always check your room before contributing.
  - q: Can I withdraw and put the money back in the same year?
    a: Only if you have unused room left. Withdrawals are added back to your room on January 1 of the next year, so re-contributing too soon can lead to a penalty.
  - q: Should I choose a TFSA or an RRSP?
    a: It depends on your income now and what you expect in retirement. A TFSA is often better at lower incomes or when you want flexibility, while an RRSP is often better at higher incomes. Many people use both, and we can help you find the right balance.

A Tax-Free Savings Account, or TFSA, is one of the most flexible ways to save in Canada. You contribute money you have already paid tax on, and from then on, **your growth and your withdrawals are tax-free**.

Despite the name, a TFSA is not just a savings account. It is a container that can hold many types of investments, so your money can work as hard as you want it to.

## How your room adds up

You start earning TFSA room in the year you turn 18, as long as you are a Canadian resident with a valid SIN. Each year, the CRA sets a new annual limit, and any room you do not use carries forward. Because the limit can change, the best place to see your exact room is **CRA My Account**.

## Choosing what goes inside your TFSA

With us, you can fill your TFSA with insurance-based options that match your goals:

- **Segregated funds:** growth potential with guarantees on your principal at maturity or death, plus the option to name a beneficiary.
- **GICs:** guaranteed returns with no market risk, ideal for short-term goals or emergency savings.
- **Successor holder:** naming your spouse lets your TFSA pass to them tax-free and keep its tax-free status.
