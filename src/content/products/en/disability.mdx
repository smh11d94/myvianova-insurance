  - q: What is an elimination period?
    a: It is the waiting time between when your disability starts and when benefits begin, often 30, 90 or 120 days. A longer wait lowers your premium, so many people match it to their emergency savings or sick leave.
  - q: I already have group disability at work. Do I need more?

Disability insurance pays you a **monthly income** if an illness or injury keeps you from working. It helps you pay the mortgage, groceries and bills while you focus on getting better.

Most people insure their home and car, but forget about their ability to earn a paycheque. Yet a serious illness or injury can happen at any age, and a long time off work can drain savings quickly.

## When disability insurance makes the most sense

If you depend on your income to pay your bills, disability insurance is worth considering. It is especially important for the self-employed, people without workplace benefits and professionals whose careers depend on specialized skills. If you have a group plan, an individual policy can top it up and stays with you if you change jobs.

## Choosing a policy

When comparing disability policies, look at more than the premium:

- **Definition of disability:** does the plan cover your own occupation, or only any occupation?
- **Waiting and benefit periods:** balance the length of the wait and the length of the benefit with your budget.
