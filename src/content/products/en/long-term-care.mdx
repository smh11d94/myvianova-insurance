Long-term care insurance helps pay for the care you may need if you can no longer look after yourself because of illness, injury, aging or a condition like dementia. It pays a **weekly or monthly benefit** to help cover care at home, in a retirement residence or in a long-term care home.

Canadians are living longer, and many of us will need some help with daily tasks later in life. That care can be expensive, and the cost often falls on savings or on family members.

## Why plan for care now

Provincial programs help, but they do not cover everything. Long-term care homes charge residents a co-payment for accommodation, waitlists can be long, and publicly funded home care may only cover a few hours a week. Long-term care insurance gives you extra money to fill those gaps and more say in where you receive care.

## What to look for in a plan

When comparing long-term care coverage, consider:

- **Benefit style:** an income plan pays a set amount, while a reimbursement plan pays back eligible expenses.
- **Waiting period and benefit length:** how long before payments start, and how long they can continue.
- **Premium guarantees:** whether your premiums are locked in or can be adjusted by the insurer.
- **Availability:** few insurers in Canada currently offer standalone long-term care plans, so we will help you find the options available to you.
